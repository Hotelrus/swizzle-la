Is There a Michelin Guide in Laos?

The short answer, how the guide actually works, why Laos is not in it yet, and where to eat in Vientiane while we wait.

Updated November 2026

Short answer

No. In 2026 there is no Michelin restaurant guide for Laos, so no restaurant in the country holds a star. Michelin does list a few Lao hotels, including one in Vientiane, and its travel guide rates Pha That Luang as worth the trip. Everything else is not covered yet.

People ask us this often, and it is a fair question with Thailand and Vietnam next door. Here is what is true as of November 2026, with links to Michelin's own pages so you can check.

What Michelin does list in Laos

Michelin runs three separate things, and people blur them together: a restaurant guide, a hotel selection and a travel guide. Laos appears in the second and third, not the first.

Hotels. In 2018 Michelin bought Tablet Hotels and turned it into the MICHELIN Guide hotel selection, which now runs to more than 7,000 hotels worldwide. The best of them get one, two or three MICHELIN Keys, a distinction added in 2024. Vientiane has exactly one hotel in it: the Lao Poet Hotel in Chanthabouly, which Michelin describes with its rooftop poolside bar and a lobby restaurant cooking an upscale take on Lao food. It is listed, without a Key. Luang Prabang does better: Amantaka holds one Key and Rosewood Luang Prabang was given three in October 2025, alongside listings for Satri House, La Résidence Phou Vao, 3 Nagas and The Namkhan. Vang Vieng has Riverside Boutique Resort.

Sights. Michelin's travel guide, the old Green Guide, has rated monuments since before it rated kitchens. Pha That Luang, the gold stupa ten minutes' walk from our door, carries its "worth the trip" rating, the highest it gives. So Michelin has already been to That Luang. It just came for the stupa, not for dinner.

Restaurants. Nothing. Michelin's inspectors do not work in Laos, and the guide's page for Lao cuisine carries only a note that new regions are added over time.

Stars, Bibs, Keys: what the words mean

The guide started in 1900 as a free booklet from the tyre company, built to get French motorists driving further. It went on sale in 1920 and began awarding a single star for good cooking in 1926, which makes 2026 the star's hundredth birthday. The one-to-three scale arrived in the early 1930s. Inspectors are full-time Michelin employees who book under assumed names, eat around 250 meals a year each and pay the bill in full, which is the part of the system worth defending.

Two things follow. A listing with no star is still a real listing; Selected and Bib Gourmand venues make up most of every guide. And a Key says nothing about the kitchen. A hotel can hold three Keys in a country where no restaurant has ever been inspected, which is exactly the situation in Laos.

Why there is no Laos guide yet

Michelin does not simply decide a country is ready and send inspectors. New destinations arrive through partnerships, usually with a national or city tourism body, and the partner pays. The numbers are public because American cities have to disclose them: California paid about 600,000 dollars for its 2019 guide, Atlanta roughly a million, Texas 2.7 million between its cities in 2024, and Minneapolis agreed 250,000 dollars a year for three years in April 2026, for a first guide in 2027. Closer to home, Vietnam's 2026 edition had Sun Group as its Destination Partner.

Michelin's position is consistent: no restaurant pays to be included, partners get no say in who is selected, and inspectors go in only once a food scene has reached "culinary maturity". The sequence is the same everywhere. A tourism authority signs, inspectors eat anonymously for a year or so, then there is a ceremony. Laos has not taken the first step.

How the neighbours compare

Thailand's 2026 guide, announced in November 2025, covers 468 venues, with Sorn and Sühring in Bangkok on three stars. Vietnam's fourth edition, announced in Hanoi on 4 June 2026, lists 193 places across Hanoi, Ho Chi Minh City and Da Nang, with a record eleven one-star restaurants and 72 Bib Gourmands. The Philippines joined in October 2025 with 108 establishments, one of them on two stars, and handed out an Exceptional Cocktails award on its first night, to Benjamin Leal of Uma Nota in Manila. Bars are watching that one closely.

Cambodia and Myanmar are where Laos is: Cambodia received its first hotel selection in October 2025, with ten Keys, but no restaurant guide. Myanmar has neither. So Laos is not behind its peers so much as in the same waiting room, and the Keys in Luang Prabang mean Michelin already has a working relationship here.

Lao food Michelin has recognised abroad

The cuisine has been inspected. It just happened in Washington DC, which has one of the largest Lao communities outside Asia. Thip Khao, opened by chef Seng Luangrath in 2014, is in the city's Michelin guide, and Laos in Town has been written up by the guide as a Bib Gourmand, with the inspectors' piece on it titled around eating Lao food for under 35 dollars. Larb, naem khao and jeow are Michelin-approved. They are just approved in another hemisphere.

Laos itself came closest in November 2018, when Bee Satongun, whose Paste in Bangkok holds a star, opened Paste Laos at The Apsara in Luang Prabang, cooking from Phia Sing's royal palace recipes. Michelin's own site covered the opening. The Paste name has since come off the door and the hotel runs the room itself, so for now the closest a starred chef has come to Laos is a visit.

Could Laos get a guide?

Yes, and the shape of it is clear. The scene has already produced one kitchen the wider region noticed: Doi Ka Noi, Ponpailin "Noi" Kaewduangdee's nine-dish, market-led Lao restaurant, became the first Lao restaurant on Asia's 50 Best extended list in February 2025. It closed later that year after her death, and the city still feels the gap. A guide would start with a tourism authority signing a multi-year partnership, probably covering Vientiane and Luang Prabang together, the way Vietnam launched with two cities. Michelin would want enough serious kitchens to fill a list: a dozen that could compete for stars, a few dozen Bib candidates and a long tail of Selected. Luang Prabang's hotel dining rooms would carry some of that weight. Vientiane would need its independents, not just its hotels, cooking at a level inspectors can defend.

What would help most is depth, not money. More cooks doing one thing properly, more rooms open seven nights, suppliers who deliver the same fish on Tuesday as on Saturday. That is true whether or not a tyre company ever sends someone to check.

Where to eat in Vientiane while we wait

Nobody needs an inspector to eat well here. Three restaurants we send people to for a proper Lao meal, with one disclosure at the end. Bars are a separate subject, covered in our Vientiane bar guide. Hours change, so check before you go.

3 Merchants

Ground floor, Crowne Plaza, Samsenthai Road · lunch and dinner weekdays, dinner at weekends

The hotel dining room that outgrew the hotel. 3 Merchants cooks the food of the old trade routes, Lao first, then Vietnamese, Cambodian, Thai and Malay, over a charcoal grill, and it has sat at or near the top of the Tripadvisor ranking for Vientiane for years. The laab here is made with care, the duck curry is the dish people come back for, and it is the one place in town where you can bring someone who needs convincing that Lao food belongs in a serious room.

Khambang Lao Food Restaurant

97/2 Khounboulom Road · 10 am to 10 pm

A third-generation family restaurant with a short menu and no interest in trends, and a Lonely Planet top choice for exactly that reason. This is the place to learn what laab, or sai oua, or a proper jeow is supposed to taste like before you judge anyone else's. Plain room, honest prices, order the sticky rice by the basket.

Phakhao Lao

In the lane by Wat Ong Teu, near the river · 11:30 am to 10 pm daily

A garden terrace down an alley near the river, built around one dish done better than anyone else does it: duck laab, sharp with lime and toasted rice. The rest of the menu is a solid tour of Lao classics and the setting does half the work. It is popular with visitors, so go early or late.

Noy's Fruit Heaven That Luang, and Swizzle

Nongbone Road, by Pha That Luang · fruit 9 am to 7 pm daily, cocktails from 7 pm from 1 December

Full disclosure: this one is ours. By day it is Noy's Fruit Heaven, fresh fruit, juices and shakes from the morning market. From 1 December the same house becomes Swizzle at night: tropical cocktails on aged rum, house-made syrups and the fruit from the counter, with food from the fire. We are a ten-minute walk from the stupa Michelin rates worth the trip. The bar, Michelin has never heard of, and that is fine for now.

Questions people ask

Does Laos have a Michelin Guide?

No. As of November 2026 Michelin publishes no restaurant selection for Laos. It lists a few Lao hotels and rates Pha That Luang in its travel guide, nothing more.

Are there any Michelin star restaurants in Vientiane?

No. Inspectors do not cover Laos, so no restaurant in Vientiane, Luang Prabang or anywhere else in the country holds a star, a Bib Gourmand or a Selected listing. Anyone claiming otherwise is confusing a hotel Key or a chef's past job with a restaurant award.

Which hotels in Laos are in the Michelin Guide?

In Vientiane, only the Lao Poet Hotel, listed without a Key. Luang Prabang has several, including Amantaka with one Key and Rosewood Luang Prabang with three. Vang Vieng has Riverside Boutique Resort.

Has Michelin ever recognised Lao food?

Yes, in Washington DC. Thip Khao and Laos in Town both appear in the city's Michelin guide, and Laos in Town has been written up as a Bib Gourmand.

Will Laos get a Michelin Guide?

Not soon. New guides follow a paid multi-year partnership with a tourism body, after Michelin judges the food scene mature enough. Laos has not announced one. We will update this page the day that changes.
