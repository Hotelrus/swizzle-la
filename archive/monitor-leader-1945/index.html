Provenance and Rights Record

Monitor-Leader 14 August 1945

The newspaper did not renew. The article inside it is syndicated copy, and the syndicate did.

ItemLCCN sn96077289
Checked2026-09-04
DeterminationNot cleared
Determination

Do not treat this clipping as public domain. The expiration argument that clears the 1941 menu does not reach it.

Two separate copyrights sit on this page. The issue copyright, belonging to the newspaper, shows no renewal: the Daily Monitor Leader does not appear anywhere in the inventory of periodicals with renewed issue or contribution copyrights for 1931 to 1950. That much is clean.

But the article is not the newspaper's own work. It is bylined "BY HARRY GRAYSON, NEA Staff Correspondent", making it syndicated copy from the Newspaper Enterprise Association, and it therefore carries a contribution copyright of its own. NEA Service Weekly has contribution renewals running from 11 June 1945. This issue is 14 August 1945, two months inside that window. Whether this specific column was among the renewed contributions has not been established, and until it is, expiration cannot be relied on.

The complication

Two copyrights on one page

Term

Where the two tracks diverge

1945Published. First term of 28 years begins on both the issue and the contribution.
Jun 1945NEA contribution renewals begin running, two months before this issue.
1972–73Renewal window. Nothing filed for the newspaper's own issue copyright.
1972NEA contribution renewals filed and recorded in the July to December volume.

The newspaper's silence in 1972 would carry the issue into the public domain. The syndicate's filing in the same year is what stops the clipping following it.

Evidence

What the searches returned

Newspapers are periodicals, Class B. The Stanford Copyright Renewal Database used for the menu covers Class A, books and pamphlets, and does not contain newspapers at all, so a zero there would have meant nothing. The instrument for this question is the Online Books Page inventory, which states it should include every periodical with renewed issue or contribution copyrights for 1931 to 1950 issues. The two control rows confirm the corpus holds Michigan dailies and holds 1945 renewals, so the absence in row one is real absence.

The blocking entry

The line that stops the clearance

NEA Service Weekly : issues renewed from © January 8, 1951;
see CPRS; contributions renewed from © June 11, 1945;
see 1972 July-December

NEA is the Newspaper Enterprise Association, the Scripps-Howard feature syndicate that employed Harry Grayson. The entry records that contributions to its service were being renewed from 11 June 1945, filed in the 1972 July to December volume of the Catalog of Copyright Entries. Our issue is 14 August 1945. That is inside the window, which is why this record ends in a caution rather than a clearance.

What we do about it

House position on this clipping

  • It stays an excerpt. A small crop of the relevant passage, at the size needed to read the sentence being discussed, inside an article about that sentence.
  • The full page does not go up. We hold the page at 5796 by 7700 pixels. It is not published, and there is no click to enlarge on this item, because reproducing the whole syndicated article is a different act from quoting six lines of it.
  • The citation stays complete. Title, date, printed page, and a link to the Library of Congress record, so a reader can verify the quotation at source.
  • This rests on commentary, not expiration. We are quoting a short passage to discuss what it says. That is a different basis from the menu, which we publish in full because its term has demonstrably run out.
Limits

What this finding does and does not establish

The newspaper's own result is solid

Two control rows confirm the corpus contains Michigan dailies, 1945 renewals, and explicit negatives. The absence of this title is absence.

The syndicate's result is not resolved

We know NEA renewed contributions in this period. We have not read the 1972 July to December volume to see whether this column is among them.

The unresolved half is the one that matters

A clean newspaper and a renewed contribution produce a clipping you still cannot publish freely. The weaker finding governs.

It could be settled

Reading the named CCE volume would resolve it either way, as would a Copyright Office search report. Neither has been done.

Correction recorded

Page number. This article previously cited the clipping as page 36. That is the Library of Congress image sequence number. The printed folio, visible in the page header beside the running head, is page 16 of the V-J edition. Corrected in the article on 4 September 2026.
